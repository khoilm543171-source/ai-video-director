# Camera Direction Skill

Use this whenever turning an approved script scene into a storyboard or video prompt.

## Required camera grammar
For every scene decide, in this order:
1. shot size: ECU / CU / MCU / MS / MWS / WS;
2. angle: eye-level, low, high, top-down, or three-quarter;
3. lens feel: wide 24-35mm, natural 35-50mm, portrait 50-85mm equivalent;
4. subject blocking: foreground / midground / background and left / center / right;
5. movement: locked, dolly, truck, pan, tilt, crane, or handheld;
6. focus behavior: deep focus, subject hold, or one rack focus;
7. screen direction and continuity anchor;
8. end-frame condition for the next cut.

## Rules
- One dominant camera idea per scene.
- Vertical 9:16 must keep the teaching subject readable in the center safe area.
- Prefer natural 35-50mm feel for dialogue and 24-35mm for engine-room geography.
- Use close-ups only for meaningful technical details.
- Avoid unnecessary orbit shots, whip pans, shaky handheld, extreme dutch angles, and camera motion that competes with teaching.
- Preserve left-right movement and machinery position across adjacent scenes unless the cut intentionally resets geography.
- Do not put camera instructions into spoken dialogue.
